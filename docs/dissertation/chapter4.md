## 4.2 Analysis and diagnostic findings

The following diagnostics were performed to explain the relative underperformance of the implemented PREDICT policy.

- Forecast–action lag: log timestamps and experiment traces indicate non‑negligible latency between feature capture, inference, and decision application. In highly time‑varying traces this lag produced stale forecasts at decision time.

- Conservative decision mapping: the implemented translation from a point forecast to an ABR representation applies strong safety margins, biasing decisions toward low representations and reducing visual quality despite fewer stalls.

- Preprocessing/scaler misalignment: a small but systematic mismatch between the scaler semantics used at training and those applied at inference produced biased predictions. The exported scaler metadata (`ml/models/final/onnx_scalers.json`) was added so the C++ predictor can reproduce training preprocessing exactly; diagnostic logs in the runtime show the scaler values read by the predictor.

### 4.2.1 Figure captions & label notes

All diagnostic figure captions that show example traces or per‑decision timelines include the note "(Week 11 representative trace)" to indicate the example provenance and to avoid over‑generalizing from a single case.

## 4.3 Discussion

The empirical evidence indicates that, as implemented, the PREDICT policy underperforms a well‑tuned MPC baseline in these experiments. This outcome is attributable to the three interacting mechanisms above rather than a fundamental limitation of predictive control.

Planned immediate remediation: a light engineering pass is planned prior to finalizing claims — specifically, align scaler usage end‑to‑end (training ↔ inference) and relax conservative decision margins; the experiments will be re‑run to verify numerical changes before the thesis is finalized.
